INTRODUCTION

Every e-commerce store has numbers. But not every store knows the real story behind them.

This project started with one question: What actually drives profit — and what kills it?

No ready-made dataset. No clean, perfect data. Because real life isn't like that. Real life: missing columns, different date formats in the same field, tables with no clear keys.

The journey wasn't a straight line. Two weeks on Kaggle — nothing worked. Every dataset was incomplete or broken. So, I made my own data. 6 tables. Built from scratch. Messed it up on purpose to simulate reality.

Behind this project: one person. No courses. No one guiding me. YouTube and AI. And a lot of patience, frustration, and stubbornness. 9 months in the field. 37 days on this specific project.

Every day: 3.5 hours. A repeating pattern: understand → document → cross-check with AI.

Not to build a "pretty dashboard" like social media sells. But to understand the full picture — the numbers, the causes, and the mistakes behind them.

CHAPTER 1: Project Vision & Objective

6 tables built from scratch • 31 days of work • One question: Why?

1.1 Why This Project?

Anyone can build a dashboard. But can they answer "Why did this happen?" I saw a gap. Most people explain what happened. Almost no one explains why. So, I built this project differently.

1.2 The Real Question

Every business sees numbers move — sales up, sales down, profit margins shrink. The question every E-commerce manager wants answered is simple: Why?

Why did sales drop in February and March? Why did profit margin change from month to month? Was it discounts? Or delivery delays? Or both? This project was my attempt to answer that — not by guessing, but by connecting the dots.

1.3 Why I Built My Own Data

Real data isn't clean. I spent two weeks on Kaggle. Every dataset was incomplete or very very broken, and more, some of it contain data integrity issue. So, I made my own. 6 tables. Built from scratch. Messed up on purpose to simulate reality. Why? Because I wanted to understand why data behaves the way it does. Not just use it.

1.4 Correlation and Causation

I learned the rule: Correlation doesn't mean causation. But in business, when you see delays increase → sales drop → and it happens more than once — something is there. You still check discounts, marketing, competitors. But at some point, I stop guessing and start connecting.

CHAPTER 2: Dataset Foundation

2.1 The 6 Tables

Before I built this project, I spent about 1.5 months deep in SQL. Not just SELECT, WHERE, GROUP BY — but also HAVING, ORDER BY, INNER JOIN, LEFT JOIN, aggregation, and how to connect all of that to a business question.

But I realized something: You can't really understand INNER JOIN or LEFT JOIN unless you understand relationships — entities, events, primary keys, foreign keys, sql engine and how NULL affects everything.

  • Entity: distinct real-world object or concept about which data is collected and stored.
  • Event: an action or occurrence that happens at a specific point in time.
  • Relationship: how two or more entities are connected.
  • Primary Key: a unique identifier for each record in a table.
  • Foreign Key: a field in one table that links to the primary key of another.

2.2 Why City and Not Branches?

I'm not a retail store with physical branches. I'm an E-commerce business. I ship from one main location (Cairo) to customers in Mansoura and Alexandria. So, I made a city table, not a Branches table. This keeps the model clean and reflects reality.
