The Journey of a Self-Directed Data Analyst

I spent the last 13 months teaching myself data analytics — not through a structured curriculum, but by building, breaking, and rebuilding until things made sense.

There were no instructors to tell me when I was wrong, so I learned to question everything myself. Overthinking evolved into critical thinking. Uncertainty became a habit of validating assumptions before trusting results. And the long hours spent decoding a single problem taught me that understanding data deeply is far more valuable than just solving a problem quickly.

I did not just learn SQL or Power BI; I learned why a metric must be defined before it is calculated, and why a benchmark must come from the data before it is applied.

I realized that the most important question in any analysis is never "how do I build this?" — it is always "what does this actually mean for the business?"

This portfolio documents that mindset. Not just the tools I used or the dashboards I built, but the thinking that developed over 13 months of navigating real business problems without a map.
