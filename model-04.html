However, analysis alone is never the final destination.
Business decisions are not made directly from dashboards or KPIs. They are made by balancing analytical evidence with the current business context and the objective the business is trying to achieve.

Model 04 — Section 03

Building the
Evidence

Reviewing the analytical findings before evaluating business alternatives.

Before discussing any business decision, it is important to verify that the available evidence actually answers the original business question.

Common Analytical Mistake
Expanding the analysis beyond the original objective.
Simply because more data is available does not mean it should be analyzed. A good analysis does not answer every possible question. It answers the right question. This project could have analyzed cities, monthly trends, or customer behavior — but none of these would have directly answered the original business question.
The Original Objective — Model 3
"How can product performance be measured fairly without relying on revenue alone?"

These metrics were combined through a weighted scoring model to produce a balanced performance evaluation. The insights below are therefore treated as evidence — not as business decisions.

Four Pieces of Evidence
01
Evidence 01
Revenue Concentration
~50% of Revenue
Mobile Devices generated approximately half of the company's total revenue, making it the strongest revenue-driving category by a significant margin.
Business Insight
The category is clearly the company's primary revenue engine — and any strategy that ignores it ignores the majority of business value.
Business Risk
Relying heavily on a single category introduces concentration risk. Any decline in demand, supply, pricing, or competition could have a disproportionate impact on overall performance.
02
Evidence 02
Product Performance Is More Than Revenue
Weighted Model
The weighted scoring model identified the Top 10 products by combining revenue, quantity sold, and purchase frequency — instead of relying on revenue alone.
Business Insight
Revenue explains only part of product performance. Products that consistently sell, maintain customer demand, and generate repeat purchases may provide greater long-term business value than products ranked by revenue alone.
03
Evidence 03
Hidden Business Value
Accessories
Although Accessories contributed a relatively small share of total revenue, several accessory products appeared within the highest-performing products after applying the weighted scoring model.
Business Insight
Lower-revenue categories can still contain strategically valuable products when evaluated using multiple business metrics. This demonstrates why balanced performance measurement provides a more realistic picture than revenue alone.
04
Evidence 04
Revenue Quality
Low Discount Ratio
The business generated strong revenue while total discounts represented only a relatively small percentage of overall sales.
Business Insight
Revenue growth was not driven primarily by aggressive discounting. This suggests that product demand contributed more to business performance than discount dependency — a sign of healthier, more sustainable revenue.
Evidence Summary
What the Analysis Proved — and What It Did Not
Model 3 successfully answered its original business question by establishing a balanced way to evaluate product performance and transforming analytical results into meaningful business insights.
However, these findings are not business decisions. They are the analytical evidence that will be used in the next stage to evaluate multiple business alternatives, compare their trade-offs, and determine which decision best fits the current business objective.
The same four findings above will now serve as the foundation for evaluating business alternatives. Analysis becomes a decision only when it is tested against context and a clear objective.
Model 04 — Section 04

Turning Evidence
into Decisions

Strong analysis does not produce a single answer. It produces several defensible decisions — each supported by the same evidence, but driven by different business priorities.

By this stage, the data has been validated, explored, and translated into reliable business evidence. The remaining challenge is no longer discovering insights, but deciding how those insights should influence the business.
Because the same evidence can support different objectives, there is rarely a single "correct" strategy. Instead, the analyst must understand the strengths, limitations, and business implications of each possible direction before making a recommendation.
01
Approach 01
Strengthen the Strongest Revenue Driver
Why This Approach?
Mobile Devices contribute nearly 50% of total revenue. The highest-performing product belongs to this category. Protecting and strengthening the strongest revenue contributor could maximize short-term business impact — this approach focuses on reinforcing what is already working.
AI-Generated · Trade-off Balance
Business Advantage
  • Higher probability of immediate revenue growth
  • Builds on an already proven product category
  • Requires less uncertainty than investing in weaker categories
Potential Trade-off
  • Increases business dependency on a single category
  • Demand shifts, competition, or supply issues could have disproportionate impact
  • Concentration risk grows with every additional investment
02
Approach 02
Build a Second Growth Pillar
Why This Approach?
Instead of increasing investment in the strongest category, the business could focus on strengthening Accessories as a second commercial pillar — creating a more balanced revenue structure rather than relying heavily on a single category.
AI-Generated · Trade-off Balance
Business Advantage
  • Reduces dependency on one dominant category
  • Creates a healthier and more diversified revenue mix
  • Makes the business less vulnerable to demand or market changes
Potential Trade-off
  • Requires more investment, more time, and greater execution effort
  • Return is less predictable than investing in an already successful category
  • Results may take longer to justify the investment
03
Approach 03
Invest in High-Potential Products
Why This Approach?
Rather than prioritizing entire product categories, investment is directed toward the highest-ranked products identified by the weighted scoring model. This strategy follows product performance instead of category performance — using data-driven prioritization instead of intuition.
AI-Generated · Trade-off Balance
Business Advantage
  • Uses data-driven prioritization instead of intuition
  • Targets products with strong overall business performance
  • Can improve return on investment without committing to a single category
Potential Trade-off
  • May overlook broader category development
  • Could create an uneven product portfolio
  • Sustainable growth often depends on a balanced ecosystem, not individual products
04
Approach 04 — The Tempting Choice
Hybrid Investment Strategy
Why It Feels Safe
Instead of choosing a single direction, distribute the investment across all three strategies. At first glance, this appears to be the safest and most balanced option — it reduces dependence on one decision and spreads investment across multiple opportunities.

AI-Generated · Trade-off Balance
Benefits
  • Diversifies investment across multiple directions
  • Reduces the risk of relying on one assumption
  • Addresses multiple business opportunities simultaneously

Risks
  • No strategy receives enough investment to create measurable impact
  • Resources become fragmented across competing priorities
  • Success becomes impossible to attribute to a specific decision
  • Splitting the budget feels safer — but often produces weaker results
The Irony
This approach is the easiest recommendation to make — and often the least decisive. When uncertainty exists, splitting the budget feels safer than committing to one direction. Ironically, that feeling of safety can be the most expensive choice of all.
Section 04 — Closing
"None of these approaches is inherently right or wrong. Each one optimizes a different business objective — and each is fully supported by the same analysis."
The analyst's responsibility is therefore not to maximize the number of recommendations, but to understand which recommendation best fits the current business context. That distinction transforms analysis from generating options into supporting decisions — which naturally leads to the next section.

Reframing the Purpose of Every Tool
SQL
Not an end. A way to extract reliable evidence from raw data.
Dashboards
Not a final product. A communication layer that translates evidence for decision makers.
Insights
Not conclusions. Starting points for better business questions.
They are tools that support a larger purpose: enabling better decisions.
The Role of the Analyst
Analysts do not make business decisions on behalf of the organization. They provide recommendations supported by reliable data, meaningful analysis, and transparent reasoning — allowing decision makers to choose based on evidence rather than assumptions. A different stakeholder, a different budget, or a different business goal could reasonably lead to a different recommendation while using the exact same evidence.
The Same Principle — AI
AI can accelerate learning, challenge assumptions, explore alternatives, and support problem-solving. However, it does not replace the analyst's responsibility to understand the business problem, validate the data, evaluate the evidence, and justify the recommendation. The thinking must remain human. The tools can change.
What Changes
  • The tools available to analysts
  • The industries being analyzed
  • The business priorities driving decisions
  • The data sources and technical platforms
What Stays
  • Disciplined analytical thinking
  • Evidence before recommendation
  • Transparency in reasoning
  • Understanding the problem before choosing the tool
The Central Idea
Not a gallery of dashboards.
Not a collection of technical skills.
A repeatable analytical mindset.
Understanding the problem. Building reliable evidence. Evaluating alternatives. Supporting better decisions. That mindset is what this portfolio was built to demonstrate — and what remains transferable across every domain, every tool, and every industry.
